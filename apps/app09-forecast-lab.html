App 9: Forecast Lab

Chapter 9 · Established Early adoption

Convergence of a tunnel ring beneath an excavation is read every hour for 75 days, with a 2 m excavation lift every 7 days from day 20. Forecasts are made every 6 hours over the last 20 days, looking ahead by the horizon you choose. Compare a Kalman filter, an autoregressive time-series model and gradient boosting, and check how often each method's 95 % band really holds the outcome.

Accuracy and honesty of the bands

Computing…

Typical error is the root mean square of forecast minus outcome. Coverage is the share of outcomes inside the 95 % band; well below 95 % means the method is over-confident. The autoregressive model and gradient boosting use conformal bands, calibrated on forecasts made between day 45 and day 55.

What to notice. Errors grow with the horizon. The Kalman filter needs no training and is easy to explain. In the book's example the classical time-series model was the most accurate. Conformal bands are honest only when the calibration period resembles the test period, so always check the coverage. Now choose "More lifts in the test period": the excavation goes deeper than anything gradient boosting saw in training, and because a tree model cannot extrapolate, its error grows and its band stops being honest. Never trust a model beyond the conditions it has seen.
