App 11: Warning Calculator

Chapter 11 · Established Early adoption

Enter where a point is, how fast it is moving and how uncertain those figures are. The calculator works out the time until each trigger level is reached, the chance of reaching the Alert Level within the warning horizon, and the escalation stage of Chapter 11. The defaults are the worked example of Section 11.5 (point SP04). A second panel tries the inverse-velocity method on an accelerating slope.

Inverse-velocity method for accelerating movement

For slopes and ground that accelerate towards failure, one divided by the velocity often falls in a straight line towards zero. Extending the line fitted to recent readings to zero gives an estimate of the failure time (Fukuzono, 1985).

Remember. A predicted exceedance is advance notice, not the formal trigger. Trigger levels come from the design and the approving authority, and a competent person reviews every warning before anyone acts.
